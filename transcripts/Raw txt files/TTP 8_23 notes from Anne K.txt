Buddhist Grounds and Paths!
 
The more we understand the paths and stages of being a Bodhisattva, the better we can rejoice in them.
 
The different paths and grounds and realizations.
 
Rejoicing in the stages gives us access to a stream of merit far greater than the normal stream we gobble up each day.
 
What is our relationship to teachings like this, like grounds and paths, that we do not understand at all?
 
Is it, "Wow! GS can explain this just like I can explain the way to Jackson Heights!"
 
Or is is it total boredome—not connecting at all, its just words.
 
Or confusion—I can't makesense of it. Feeling unhappy and agitated which can turn into anger and frustration. The confusion makes us feel bad so then we lash out.
 
Or intellectually formed garbage—What's the purpose of this? This isn't relevant to my life. It's not practical.
 
Interesting to ID these reactions. And be clear that they are functioning and address them head on to make the dharma work.
 
We need to push the boundary of what we can accomplish. Sometimes we accept what secular society says is the limit. Which is the lowest common denominator.
 
Knowing what is possible is the first step.
 
Confusion is useful. It means my mind is encountering something I don't understand. If I only encounter what I understand and don't endure the discomfort of confusion I will never grow.
 
If I wasnt confused I'd have total understanding already. So we need to go through it. Its an improvement past not knowing about it at all.
 
Developing even doubts about emptiness destroys our samsara.
 
Anger could be fromour low self-esteem around learning becuase of an ordinary intelligence issue.
 
Intelligence is not viritue! It is neutral. Same as height or good vision. You could use it to ruin your life.
 
The virtues don't depend on our ordinary intelligence. They depened on our wisdom. Our spiritual intelligence to be developed.
 
Boredom comes from a couple places:
 
Distraction. If we are distracted dharma is very boring. A pot turned upside down.
 
Good to do breathing meditation before reading dharma. Our problem may be distraction.
 
Or we can feel we are connecting with our spiritual guide. An englightened being.
 
Sometimes its a terminological issue. If we don't know what the terms mean there is no information. In those cases there is a scholarly job—to get some more information.
 
Or pure discouragement—that's too hard. Too far away.
 
Imputing "I" on a Buddha's boyd and mind and never stopping that—that's when you are a buddha.
 
Enlightement is so close! These grounds and paths are so close!
